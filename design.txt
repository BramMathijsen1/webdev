LMML: KEY DESIGN DECISIONS
==========================
The important design decisions of LMML, each with its reason. Keep this file short: add or change a line only
for a decision that matters and is specific to this project. Last updated: 2026-10-17.


WHAT LMML IS
- A companion web app for walking tours of film and TV locations in Paris and London, told as stories, on a Leaflet
  map with the visitor's live position, in switchable themes. An end-of-course project for Information Modeling
  and Web Technologies (University of Bologna).


ARCHITECTURE
- A static site for GitHub Pages: no server, no build step, no npm. Every URL is relative (the site lives under
  /webdev/).
- Backend: backend/db.json (the database) and backend/api.js, a pure function handle(db, path) → {status, body}
  with read-only REST-style routes (/regions, /narratives, /locations/<id>, /sources). js/apiClient.js loads db.json
  once and calls it, so the pages read data as from a web API, and a real server could replace it unchanged.
- HTML first: each HTML page holds its own structure, with data-field slots that its module fills from the API.
  js/page.js builds the frame all pages share (story, theme and language menus, footer, settings), so the frame is
  written once, not copied into six pages.
- The visitor's settings (a theme picked by hand, the text cell, the active story) are kept in localStorage.


DATA
- 32 locations, 15–20 per city as the brief asks: Paris 17 (Amélie, Emily in Paris, and monuments seen on screen),
  London 15 (Love Actually, Sherlock). The Devil Wears Prada and Fleabag were removed by the owner's decision.
- db.json is normalised: records point to each other by id; joins and totals are computed in api.js.
- Every location has the same core (address, position, identifiers, facts, scenes, media, texts, references,
  cataloguing), plus tailored blocks only where they fit (heritage, architecture, business, transport, access).
- Each kind of fact uses the standard that suits it: schema.org types, CSL-JSON references, EXIF GPS fields for the
  camera's position and bearing, IPTC and Dublin Core for pictures, Wikidata and OpenStreetMap ids, the Mérimée and
  NHLE heritage registers.
- Facts first: every fact cites a source, the texts are written from those facts only, and camera data that is not
  documented stays null (the map then shows a dot without a viewing wedge). All records are drafts.
- Pictures: 31 freely licensed Commons photos and 26 credited film stills (at most 960 px wide, never cropped; three
  Sherlock stills were not found). 13 clips from official or licensed YouTube channels only, loaded on click.
- A restricted HTML vocabulary: a text uses only p, em, strong, q, cite, a and span; the pages use the short list in
  css/base.css, styled as what each element is, so a page of plain HTML (about.html) needs no classes.


TEXT GRID
- 9 texts per location (3 tones × 3 lengths), stored as HTML. A passage meant for one competence level is a
  span[data-level], so the 9 texts cover all 27 cells. Word targets: brief ≤ 60, mid 120–200, long ≥ 300.
- All 9 texts are in the page. The text section names the cell to show in data-tone, data-competence and
  data-length, and nine CSS rules hide everything else. Each of the six switches changes one attribute, and is
  disabled, with a tooltip, at the end of its axis. The young tone adds games, the scholar tone the references.


STORIES
- A story decides which locations appear and in which order, the directions, how to recognise each stop, the
  chapter introductions, the narration, the pictures, the starting text cell and the theme. Choosing a story
  applies all of it at once (Automatic).
- Two Parisiennes: Alice's class trip, young texts, Affiche. Amélie, scene by scene: Script. Shot for shot: Bruno
  and Beatrix, with camera set-ups and costume notes, Script. The game is on: a Sherlock trail for children, Affiche.
- A location page follows the active story, so a scanned QR code opens as a stop of the story being followed.


THEMES AND THE CANVAS
- A theme is CSS only: themes/<id>/theme.css, listed in themes/themes.json. It sets the fonts, colours, layout and
  order, picture framing and printed page; replacing a theme needs no JavaScript. Each theme.css opens with its
  rationale.
- Affiche, after 1920s–30s travel posters (Cassandre, the London Underground): visual first. The pictures lead, with
  flat inks per city, the route as a railway line and the record as a timetable; it prints on A4.
- Script, after screenplays and production paperwork: text first. Courier at one size, sluglines with the stop
  number in both margins, chapters on revision-page colours, storyboard frames and a call sheet; it prints on
  US Letter.
- The canvas is exactly the size of the viewport, and only the panels scroll, each in its own container. In
  landscape the panels stand side by side: three columns on screens wider than the golden ratio (1.618 : 1), two
  on squarer ones, arranged by each theme. In portrait they stand in a row, one at a time, with tabs; a phone on its
  side shows two. On paper the canvas flows onto pages.
- Proportions follow the golden ratio φ ≈ 1.618, one rule for the whole site: in every grid the lead panel is φ
  times as wide as each other panel; titles grow between steps of a golden type scale (17, 27.5, 44.5 and 72 px,
  each φ times the one before); the line height is φ. Script keeps one type size, so there φ also sets the left
  margin (φ times the right one) and the dialogue column (the line length divided by φ).
- The page stays hidden until its theme has loaded, so it never flashes in the wrong one.


PAGES
- Start, story (overview, route, map), location or story stop (text, pictures, map, record), QR codes, about, and
  colophon (the course disclaimer, listing every source from the data via /sources).
- The map shows the numbered stops, each camera position as a wedge (its bearing and lens width) and the visitor's
  position; walking times come from OSRM, computed once and stored.


LANGUAGE
- The texts are written in English, the default. A language menu (translate.js, vendored) translates a page into
  French, Dutch, German, Spanish or Italian through Microsoft's translation service, only when a visitor asks.


RISKS
- Film stills are copyrighted and the site is public: decide before publishing whether to commit them (fallback:
  git-ignore frontend/media/*/stills/, and the pages show placeholders).
- Machine translation varies in quality, the clips depend on YouTube, and every record still needs an on-site check.
